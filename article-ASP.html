Analysis of A Separate Peace

By Hyunchae Jung Jul 2025 6 min read

How does jealousy take place in A Separate Peace?

As Fulton J Sheen once said, jealousy is the tribute mediocrity pays to genius. This quote suggests that jealousy is about how average people emotionally react to greatness they cannot achieve. Instead of admiration, they respond with resentment. In A Separate Peace by John Knowles, jealousy drives the main conflict that Gene Forrester feels with Finny. This emotion quietly builds inside Gene and eventually leads to a tragic consequence.

At the beginning of the novel, Gene and Finny appear to be close friends. However, Gene starts to compare himself to Finny and secretly feels a sense of jealousy. Finny is confident and athletic, and breaks school rules, somehow avoiding punishment. Everyone seems to admire him. Gene, on the other hand, is more serious and focused on academics. He suddenly begins to believe that Finny is trying to hold him back by distracting him from his studies. When Finny starts the Suicide Club and dares Gene to jump from the tree into the water as a ritual, Gene begins to think Finny is sabotaging him by trying to pull him away from his academic success. Gene’s judgement is clouded by his jealousy.

His jealousy ultimately leads to tragic consequences for Finny. One day, while he and Finny are on the tree limb and Finny is ready to jump, Gene shakes the branch. Finny falls and shatters his leg. In that moment, Gene lets his jealousy take control of him. Although the act seems spontaneous, it’s the result of everything Gene has been feeling inside up to that point. Even though this accident shattered Finny’s entire athletic future, Gene feels relieved after this fall. He returns to the dormitory and puts on Finny’s sweater. He thinks that with Finny injured, they are finally equal, but this equality is false. Finny never saw Gene as a rival, and he never tried to compete. Even after the accident, Finny refuses to believe that Gene caused it on purpose. As the story continues, when Finny realizes the concealed truth, the emotional damage is more severe than the physical damage, ultimately leading to Finny’s death.

At the end of the novel, Gene reflects on the war and on Finny’s death. He realizes that his real enemy was not Finny, but the part of himself that couldn’t accept Finny’s goodness. Finny had no hatred, no jealousy, and no fear. When Gene says, “I never killed anybody and I never developed an intense level of hatred for the enemy. Because my war ended before I ever put on a uniform,” he is talking about the emotional war between boys of jealousy and guilt, but not the actual World War II.

The tragic realtionship between Gene and Finny in A Separate Peace shows how jealousy can destroy even the closest friendships. Instead of celebrating Finny’s greatness, Gene envies the person he loves the most, leading to a tragic result. His emotional war was stronger than any war he would ever face.
